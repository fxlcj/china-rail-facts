# China Railway Rules & Fees — verified facts, with sources and check dates

A reference for the China railway rules that foreign travelers most often get wrong: when tickets go on sale, what a refund costs, how many times a ticket can be changed, what you may carry, and what children and students pay.

Every figure below carries **the official Chinese source that states it** and **the date it was last checked**. If you copy a number anywhere, copy the check date with it — that is what makes the number verifiable at all.

- `facts.json` — the same 40 entries, machine-readable
- The human-readable version, with worked examples: <https://jiunotes.top/china-train-rules-fees-cheatsheet>
- How entries are kept current: see [Maintenance](#maintenance)

## Why this exists

English-language guides to Chinese trains are usually written once, from memory, and then age silently. Refund percentages get quoted from a rule that changed years ago. The advance-sale window is given as 30 or 60 days because that is what other countries do. Fares are described as fixed, when China moved to floating fares.

The rules themselves are not secret — China Railway and the government portals publish them, in Chinese. The gap is that almost nobody ties the English sentence back to the Chinese source, or says when they last looked. This file exists to close that gap.

**Scope, and a note on honesty.** These are published rules and fee schedules. They are not travel advice, they are not a booking service, and this is not an official publication. Where a rule could not be confirmed against an official page, it is not listed here — a shorter list beats a guessed number.

## Notes on the trickier rules

### Ticket release window and purchase channels

The most common planning error: people assume a 30- or 60-day window. It is 15 days, sale day included.

### Refund fees (cancellation)

Four tiers, and the boundary is departure time, not booking time.

### Change fees (rebooking)

One change per ticket, and the fee depends on direction — moving your trip *earlier* is free at any point.

### Luggage allowance and restricted items

Weight and dimension limits are per piece, and the carry-on size limit is tighter on high-speed (EMU) trains than on conventional ones.

### Child fares

Free under 6 without a seat, half fare ages 6–13, full fare from 14.

### Student fares

Four discounted one-way trips per academic year, and the year runs October to September.

### ID and document requirements

The English 12306 site accepts foreign passports only. Other document types work at station windows.

### Waitlist limits

The waitlist is the fallback when a train shows as sold out, and it has hard caps.

### Published fares and fare caps

China moved from fixed to floating fares in 2026; a published fare is a cap, not a price.

### Recently opened high-speed lines

New lines change what is reachable as a day trip. These three opened on 26 December 2025.

## What is *not* in this file

- **Live prices for a specific route on a specific date.** Those move with demand and seat class, and they are only authoritative inside the 12306 system at the moment of purchase.
- **Timetables.** Same reason.
- **Visa, entry and health rules.** Different authority, different change cadence; conflating them is how travel content goes wrong.
- **Anything we could not verify.** If no official Chinese page states it, it is not here.

## Maintenance

This dataset is not written once and abandoned:

- Every source URL is fingerprinted and re-fetched **daily**. When a page's content changes, the entry is flagged for a human re-check rather than silently updated.
- Each entry carries a `recheck_by` date in `facts.json`. Entries approaching that date surface as a re-check task.
- If a figure changes, its `checked_on` date changes with it. A number is never quietly swapped while the old date stays in place.

Two requests, both about accuracy rather than credit:

1. **Carry the check date with the figure.** A refund percentage without a date is not information — it is a rumor with a number attached.
2. **For anything time-sensitive, link the official source we list, not this file.** Our row is a pointer; the Chinese source is the authority.

## Machine-readable mirror & schema

`facts.json` is also served through a CDN, which helps when raw.githubusercontent.com is slow or blocked:

- <https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fxlcj/china-rail-facts@main/facts.json>

Field names differ between our repositories. This repo uses `claim_en` / `claim_zh` and a `source` object with `name` and `url`; `china-food-rules-facts` and `china-festival-calendar-facts` use a flat `claim` / `source_url` pair. Every entry in every repo carries the same three essentials: the claim, the official source URL, and the check date. See `gaps.md` for what we could not verify and what we deliberately left out.

The official source URL in each row remains the authority. The CDN is only a copy of `facts.json`.
